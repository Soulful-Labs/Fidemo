FOCUS INSITE Internal Team Console

Every step in the platform workflow that a person on our side has to finish, and the screen where they finish it. Eighteen steps in the workflow sit with the team. Everyone sees the same console, because the job is clearing the queue, and what each person did is recorded in the audit log rather than restricted in advance.

Green, nothing waiting on us Red, waiting on someone here

How work reaches the team

The platform runs on its own until it hits something it cannot decide. At that moment four things happen, in this order.

1 A step cannot finish on its own An automatic check fails, or the workflow says a person has to decide. An ID check comes back inconclusive. A screener answer is a near miss. A payout is waiting to be released.
→
2 It turns red on the dashboard, and the team is notified The study turns red, the item lands in the right queue with the person attached, and a notification goes at the same time. Nothing waits for someone to think to go and look.
→
3 Someone on the team opens it and decides Approve, adjust, reject, release, or place the person into the study by hand. The decision is always a person's. The platform never makes it.
→
4 It turns green and the study moves on The decision is written back, recorded in the audit log, and the client or participant is told. The study only goes green once nothing at all is waiting on us.

The six screens used every day

Each one exists because the workflow reaches a point the platform cannot decide on its own.

01

Dashboard

The one screen to open in the morning. Every live study on a row, green if nothing is waiting on us, red if something is. The row says what is waiting and who it is waiting on, so nobody has to go hunting through queues to find out where a study is stuck.

What turns a study red

  • A business or identity check the platform could not resolve
  • A flagged account waiting on a decision
  • A shortlist not yet confirmed
  • A borderline screener answer held for review
  • A payout or an invoice waiting to go out
  • A study that did not reach its number

What the team does here

  • Sees every stuck study in one place, without opening each one
  • Clicks the row and lands on the exact screen that clears it
  • Watches it turn green once nothing is left waiting
  • Sees fill progress and what has been charged on each study

02

Study detail

The workhorse. One study, six tabs, everything about it in one place.

Tabs

  • Setup and screener. The AI draft, the team's edits, the client's approval, and re-pricing when the screener changes
  • Pricing. The bid, the estimated incidence level, the team's adjustment, the confirmed price
  • Recruiting. Who the panel already holds, what has to be sourced outside, and the one working day answer on whether the shortfall can be filled

 

  • Applicants. Who passed the pre-screener, who terminated, and the borderline answers held for review
  • Sessions. Bookings, links, attendance codes, no-shows and reschedules
  • Payouts and delivery. The payout list, the client's approval, and the release of results

03

Exceptions queue

One queue, several types, because they all need the same thing: a person deciding.

What lands here

  • A business check where one of the four did not pass
  • An identity check that could not resolve, or a profession with no public register
  • Duplicate accounts, rushed answers and bot activity, flagged but never blocked automatically
  • Borderline screener answers held for review rather than rejected

 

  • Screening the team has to do itself
  • Phone checks, for a first specialist study or answers that do not match
  • No-shows and reschedules
  • Studies that did not reach their number

Participants

Search, profile, and the two actions only the team may take.

What it shows

06

Pricing admin

So a price can be changed without a developer.

Editable

  • The pricing sheet, which is the basis for every bid
  • The incidence bands, the probability of finding these people
  • The Focus Insite fee applied on top

Why it is here

  • The AI bid engine prices from these rules with nobody involved
  • The team adjusts the bid where a study is harder than the rules assume
  • A rule that is wrong is wrong on every study until someone fixes it

The other four, and why each has to exist

Opened less often. Each one is here because without it a specific step in the workflow has nowhere to happen.

07

Client accounts

Why: a client whose business check fails cannot request a study until a person approves them. Without this screen they sit blocked with nobody looking.

The decision

  • Approve or reject a client where one of the four checks did not pass

The record it holds

  • Terms, privacy policy and NDA signed at sign-up
  • The study contract, signed before the price is agreed
  • Card on file, and any credit carried forward
From the workflowSteps 1, 2, 3 and 8
08

Sessions and moderation

Why: no code means no payment. Somebody has to issue the link, assign the moderator and confirm who actually attended, or nobody gets paid.

The decision

  • Who moderates, and who is marked as having attended
  • What happens on a no-show or a reschedule

The record it holds

  • The code entered by both sides
  • The recording and transcript, for the client only
From the workflowSteps 38 to 44
09

Support

Why: a ticket is first answered automatically from a fixed set of FAQs and platform guides. Anything those do not cover comes to a person, and there are response times attached to it.

The decision

  • Answering what the FAQs could not cover, from either side

Against the clock

  • One working day for clients
  • Two for participants
  • Anything about money, one day

10

Audit and compliance

Why: everyone can do everything, so this log is the only record of who did what. It is also what proves the biometric obligations are being met.

What it records

  • Every reveal of a participant's contact details
  • Who changed a price, released a payout or overrode a check

What it proves

  • Written consent for the ID and the selfie
  • A published retention schedule and a deletion date
